\begin{abstract}
Adaptive query allocation in \hutchpp\ requires balancing the improvement in a
randomized low-rank approximation against the loss of residual trace samples.
We study this balance through exact rank-aware accounting and realized,
probe-specific conditional risk. We derive a local criterion for accepting an
additional basis direction, relate residual energy to dominant-subspace
capture, and construct a pilot transcript that cannot distinguish a captured
spectral knee from a missed leading direction. Frozen synthetic experiments
show that rare range-capture failures can move finite empirical mean-risk
minimizers beyond the ideal spectral knee, even when the sampled basis has
full numerical rank. Direct sample-variance estimates provide useful
empirical discrimination between realized Rademacher risks, but charging for
this information can worsen most individual paths while improving aggregate
mean risk. We prove conditional safe-acceptance statements and identify
small-budget vacuity in two explicit confidence constructions. A paired
common-probe formulation and a structural-prior bound clarify what remains
unresolved. These results characterize limitations of adaptive allocation
and certification; they do not establish a uniformly superior allocator or
an impossibility theorem for all small-budget certificates.
\end{abstract}
